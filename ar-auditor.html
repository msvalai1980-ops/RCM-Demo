Priority Mix — Is the Team Working the Right Accounts?
What this is: what share of this period's follow-ups went to Priority 1, Priority 2, Priority 3, versus claims touched before they were actually due. A "too-early" follow-up is when an agent re-touches a claim before the date its last note said to re-check it — wasted effort on a claim that wasn't ready to move, instead of working something that actually needed attention.

Action-Match Audit — Stated Action vs. Verified Outcome
What this is: for each action an agent logged on a claim, does the claim's actual current state confirm it really happened — e.g. does "Claim Rebilled" mean a rebill is really on file? A mismatch means the note doesn't match reality (the rebill was never submitted, the appeal was never filed). The last row is claims the audit itself flagged outright as a likely incorrect follow-up.

Timeliness Audit — Stale Follow-ups
What this is: the flip side of "too early" above. If a note says "re-check in 10 days" and nobody has touched that claim in 18 days, it's stale — actionable right now, but nobody's working it. It's bucketed by how overdue, since the longer it sits the more it risks timely-filing or appeal-window deadlines. In most A/R teams, stale claims (not too-early ones) are the bigger dollar leak.

Outcome-to-Cash Audit — Did "Collectible" Actually Convert?
What this is: for claims logged as Collectible above, did money actually follow? Within 30 days of that note, checks whether the claim posted a payment or adjustment (Converted), is still waiting on the payer (Still Pending), or never moved (No Conversion) — this is what separates an optimistic note from an outcome that actually paid.

Note-Completeness Audit
What this is: whether the note an agent logged actually captured the information that action type requires to be useful later — a "Claim Rebilled" note with no new claim number, an "Appeal Submitted" note with no confirmation ID. Missing that detail makes the note hard to verify or follow up on down the line.

Escalation-Compliance Audit
What this is: claims that trip an escalation rule (e.g. Priority 1, balance over a threshold, no resolution after N touches) should get moved up to a supervisor or specialist rather than sit with the same agent indefinitely. By team: how many claims met that trigger, how many actually got escalated, and the resulting compliance rate. Catches high-dollar claims quietly aging out because nobody raised their hand on them.
